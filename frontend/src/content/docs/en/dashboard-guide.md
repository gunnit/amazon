# Guide to the sections

The left-hand menu is split into three groups: **Analytics** (the numbers), **Intelligence** (the readings and suggestions) and **Operations** (the management). Below, for each entry, what it is for and which question it answers.

## Analytics

**Dashboard** — The overall snapshot: revenue, orders, trend over the selected period. This is the screen to open to understand in thirty seconds how the client is doing.

**Performance** — The product-by-product detail. It answers: which items are pulling, which are declining, where revenue is concentrated.

**Advertising** — The Amazon campaigns: spend, sales generated, return. It answers: how much am I spending, what is paying off and what is burning budget.

**Forecasts** — The estimate of future sales, built on historical data. It helps you plan stock and budget. If history is short or very irregular, the platform tells you: read the forecast as a rough indication in that case.

## Intelligence

**Market Research** — Positioning within the category: prices, competitors, where the brand stands versus the others. It answers: who is ahead of me and why.

**Brand Analysis** — The in-depth brand analysis, which also produces the presentation you can take to the client.

**Weekly Brand Intelligence** — The week in review: what changed in the last seven days and what deserves attention now.

**Recommendations** — Operational suggestions generated by the platform, in priority order. It answers: if I have time for three things this week, which ones do I do.

## Operations

**Accounts** — The list of connected Amazon accounts, with their status and the progress of the historical load. This is where you connect a new account and where you find out whether an account has a problem.

**Catalog** — The products synced from Amazon: codes, titles, prices. Use it to check that everything is there and that the data matches Amazon.

**Alerts** — Automatic notifications: sales drops, competitor price changes, anomalies. This is where you find them even when email sending is not active.

**Settings** — The Amazon credentials (including the client secret field), preferences and organisation configuration.

## Comparing two periods

Almost everywhere you can pick a date range and compare it with a previous period, to see percentage changes instead of just absolute values.

1. Open the date selector at the top of the screen.
2. Pick the period you are interested in. Inside the calendar you can click on the month and the year to **jump straight** to a distant date, without scrolling back month by month.
3. Set the comparison period.
4. Click **Apply**.

Data only refreshes after you click **Apply**: if you changed the dates and nothing seems to move, it is almost always that missing click.

## Exporting to Excel, PowerPoint and PDF

From the export screens you generate the file and download it to your computer:

- **Excel** for numbers you want to rework;
- **PowerPoint** for the client presentation, already laid out;
- **PDF** for a document to attach or print.

Exporting is always available and does not depend on email reports.
